            <p>
                When the United States enters into a conflict overseas or when major conflicts arise overseas where the United States has allies, troops will be sent. There is a lot that goes into deciding how many troops to send and how to escalate. The National Command Authority is the ultimate legal source of military orders. It consists jointly of the President and the Secretary of Defense. According to Brigadier General Gregory Touhill, "The orders of the President are transmitted through the Secretary of Defense across the Department of Defense." There have been conflicts that the US got involved in both more than 70 years ago as well as less than 20 yearss ago that have led to major spikes in troop deployment. 
            </p>

            <p>
                The Vietnam War saw many significant spikes in troops. When it comes to escalating troop deployment, General Touhill says, "the War Powers Resolution Act comes into play. If the issue is not resolved in the first hundred days, then the National Command Authority has to go to Congress for consultation." This shows an extension of the hierarchy when the National Command Authority can't deal with a problem in a certain amount of time.
            </p>
